## de-starter-01: Ask for a drink

Outcome: Build a request with “I would like …”.
Linked existing lesson: de-lesson01. Version: 1.0.0. Review status: awaiting-human-review.
Recommended prerequisites: None. All activities remain unlocked.

### Lesson

#### 1. A quick starting point (prior; prior)

Have you learned German before? This helps us avoid crediting the lesson for something you already know. You can choose “I don’t know yet”.

Companion, shown once: “We can start from the beginning.” During an attempt it remains still and adds no unsolicited guidance.

Learner prompt: If you already can, ask for water in a full German sentence. Otherwise leave this blank.

#### 2. One useful request (idea; teach)

ich means I. möchte means would like. Tee means tea. Put them together in that order: Ich möchte Tee. You are making a request, not saying that you already have tea. German writes Tee with a capital letter because it is a noun. We will change just the drink first.

Companion, shown once: “Let’s work out one small pattern.” During an attempt it remains still and adds no unsolicited guidance.

New/recalled pieces: ich = I; möchte = would like; Tee = tea.

Model: **Ich möchte Tee.** — I would like tea.. Optional audio uses device speech; never autoplays.

#### 3. Check the meaning (meaning; understand)

What is the speaker doing with “Ich möchte Tee”?

Meaning choices: Requesting tea / Saying they already have tea.

Requested hints, in order:

1. möchte describes what the person would like.

Answer available on request: Requesting tea

#### 4. Change just the drink (water-word; teach)

Wasser means water. Keep the first two pieces of the request. You will choose the final piece yourself.

New/recalled pieces: Wasser = water.

#### 5. Make your own request (water; construct)

The examples are put away. Take the time you need.

Companion, shown once: “You have the pieces. Try putting them together.” During an attempt it remains still and adds no unsolicited guidance.

Requested hints, in order:

1. Start with the person making the request. What comes after “I”?
2. Use “would like” before the drink in this pattern.

## de-starter-02: Say what you want to do

Outcome: Put an action at the end of a request.
Linked existing lesson: de-lesson06. Version: 1.0.0. Review status: awaiting-human-review.
Recommended prerequisites: de-starter-01. All activities remain unlocked.

### Lesson

#### 1. The pieces we will reuse (recap; teach)

ich means I; möchte means would like. Put the drink after them. German writes the names of things with a capital letter. In this pattern, English and German put these pieces in the same order. That does not hold for every German sentence.

New/recalled pieces: Wasser = water; Kaffee = coffee; bitte = please.

Model: **Ich möchte Tee.** — I would like tea.. Optional audio uses device speech; never autoplays.

#### 2. Give the action its place (action; teach)

trinken means to drink. Start with Ich möchte, add the drink, and put trinken at the end. In this pattern, German puts the action after the drink. English puts “drink” before it. You do not need an extra German word for “to” here.

New/recalled pieces: trinken = to drink.

Model: **Ich möchte Tee trinken.** — I would like to drink tea.. Optional audio uses device speech; never autoplays.

#### 3. Build a new combination (water-action; construct)

Use only the words and pattern you have met.

Learner prompt: Say that you would like to drink water.

Requested hints, in order:

1. Find the words for “I would like” first.
2. Put the drink or language after möchte. Put the action last.
3. Ich möchte …

Answer available on request: Ich möchte Wasser trinken.

Assessment target: `{"subject":"ich","drink":"Wasser","action":"trinken"}`. Accept surface variants through the bounded validator. Record requested/diagnostic support before classifying evidence.

#### 4. Use the pattern for learning (learning; teach)

lernen means to learn. Deutsch means German. The action still goes at the end. You are saying what you would like to do, not what you are doing right now.

New/recalled pieces: lernen = to learn; Deutsch = German.

Model: **Ich möchte Deutsch lernen.** — I would like to learn German.. Optional audio uses device speech; never autoplays.

#### 5. A language you already know (english-word; teach)

Englisch means English. Like Deutsch, it goes before lernen in this pattern.

New/recalled pieces: Englisch = English.

## de-starter-03: Ask someone politely

Outcome: Make a polite question with möchten Sie.
Linked existing lesson: de-lesson09. Version: 1.0.0. Review status: awaiting-human-review.
Recommended prerequisites: de-starter-01, de-starter-02. All activities remain unlocked.

### Lesson

#### 1. The pieces we will reuse (recap; teach)

ich means I; möchte means would like. Put the drink after them. German writes the names of things with a capital letter. In this pattern, English and German put these pieces in the same order. That does not hold for every German sentence.

New/recalled pieces: Wasser = water; Kaffee = coffee; bitte = please.

Model: **Ich möchte Tee.** — I would like tea.. Optional audio uses device speech; never autoplays.

#### 2. An action at the end (action-recap; teach)

trinken means to drink; lernen means to learn. Deutsch is German and Englisch is English. After Ich möchte, put the drink or language first and the action last.

New/recalled pieces: trinken = to drink; lernen = to learn; Deutsch = German; Englisch = English.

Model: **Ich möchte Tee trinken.** — I would like to drink tea.. Optional audio uses device speech; never autoplays.

Model: **Ich möchte Deutsch lernen.** — I would like to learn German.. Optional audio uses device speech; never autoplays.

#### 3. A respectful way to say “you” (formal; teach)

Sie, written with a capital S, is a polite or formal “you”. Use möchten with Sie, rather than möchte. A statement is Sie möchten Tee. To ask a neutral yes/no question, put möchten first: Möchten Sie Tee? We are using this polite form with someone we do not know; informal du has its own forms.

New/recalled pieces: Sie = you, polite/formal; möchten = would like, with Sie.

Model: **Sie möchten Tee.** — You would like tea.. Optional audio uses device speech; never autoplays.

Model: **Möchten Sie Tee?** — Would you like tea?. Optional audio uses device speech; never autoplays.

#### 4. Offer someone water (water-offer; construct)

Use the polite question pattern you have just met. The examples are put away.

Learner prompt: Ask a visitor: “Would you like water?”

Requested hints, in order:

1. Which piece comes first in the question?
2. Use möchten before Sie.
3. Möchten Sie … ?

Answer available on request: Möchten Sie Wasser?

Assessment target: `{"subject":"sie","drink":"Wasser","question":true}`. Accept surface variants through the bounded validator. Record requested/diagnostic support before classifying evidence.

#### 5. Keep the action at the end (longer-question; teach)

The question starts with möchten Sie. If there is another action, it stays at the end. Only the first verb moves to the front in this pattern.

Model: **Möchten Sie Tee trinken?** — Would you like to drink tea?. Optional audio uses device speech; never autoplays.

#### 6. Ask someone politely (english-question; construct)

Use only the words and pattern you have met.

Learner prompt: Ask someone politely whether they would like to learn English.

Requested hints, in order:

1. Start with the verb to make this a polite question.
2. Use möchten before Sie. Keep the action at the end.
3. Möchten Sie … ?

Answer available on request: Möchten Sie Englisch lernen?

Assessment target: `{"subject":"sie","language":"Englisch","action":"lernen","question":true}`. Accept surface variants through the bounded validator. Record requested/diagnostic support before classifying evidence.

#### 7. Ask someone politely (coffee-question; construct)

Learner prompt: Ask someone politely whether they would like to drink coffee.

#### 8. A short reply is useful too (reply; teach)

ja means yes; nein means no; danke means thank you. Ja, bitte accepts an offer. Nein, danke declines politely. These useful replies are phrases; recalling one is different evidence from building a new sentence.

New/recalled pieces: ja = yes; nein = no; danke = thank you.

Model: **Ja, bitte.** — Yes, please.. Optional audio uses device speech; never autoplays.

Model: **Nein, danke.** — No, thank you.. Optional audio uses device speech; never autoplays.

#### 9. Choose your meaning (reply-meaning; understand)

You do not want the offered tea. Which reply fits?

Meaning choices: Nein, danke. / Ja, bitte..

Requested hints, in order:

1. Choose the reply that says no.

Answer available on request: Nein, danke.
